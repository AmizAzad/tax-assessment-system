1. Executive summary

The Tax Assessment Platform is software a tax authority uses to assess what a taxpayer owes and to carry that assessment through to its end. It covers the whole life of an assessment: choosing who to examine, gathering the evidence, working out the adjustments, calculating the liability, having it reviewed and approved by different people, issuing and serving the legal notice, handling the taxpayer's objection and any appeal, reassessing where a decision requires it, and finally settling, closing, writing off or time-barring the case.

It is built so that each country's tax rules are configuration, not software. Rates, bands, deadlines, notice wording, approval limits, reference lists, calendars and risk rules are data. The United Kingdom (Corporation Tax) is the first jurisdiction; Saudi Arabia (Corporate Income Tax) has been configured alongside it to prove that a second country can be added without rewriting the platform.

2. What the platform does

An assessment moves through fourteen stages. The table describes each in plain terms; section 4 shows every path between them.

#StageWhat happens
1InitiationThe authority decides to examine a taxpayer, a tax and a period — by a supervisor's decision, from a risk-based selection run, or automatically. One open case per taxpayer, tax and year is enforced.
2Evidence retrievalThe platform gathers the filed return and the taxpayer's account (payments, credits, losses), then freezes that evidence with a digital fingerprint so the facts a decision rested on can later be shown to be unchanged. If a mandatory source cannot answer, the case waits rather than being assessed on nothing.
3AssignmentA supervisor assigns the case to a named assessor. The case receives its reference number.
4PreparationThe assessor records adjustments to what the taxpayer declared — each with a type, a reason and a narrative — and can ask the taxpayer for information, which pauses the case until a reply or the due date.
5CalculationThe server calculates the liability through a fixed sequence: assessed base, loss set-off, taxable base, tax at the applicable rates, surcharge, credits, penalties, interest and net position. Every step is stored with its working.
6ReviewA different officer reviews the work. They accept it or return it for rework with a written reason. The preparer can never review their own work.
7ApprovalThe case is routed to an approver according to the amount at stake, never by choice. The approver approves or rejects with a reason. While a case is under review or approval its figures are locked, so the approver approves exactly what the reviewer saw.
8FinalisationThe figure becomes the legal determination. Losses the calculation relied on are consumed, so the same loss can never relieve two assessments.
9NoticeThe assessment notice is produced from approved wording (which can insert values but never calculate), stored as a PDF and given a fingerprint.
10ServiceThe notice is served by e-mail, portal, post, hand delivery or publication. The legal "deemed served" date is worked out from each country's rules, and the taxpayer's statutory window opens.
—Taxpayer's responseThe taxpayer pays (the platform concludes this from the account, nobody can simply mark it paid), objects, or lets the window lapse. A supervisor may write off an uncollectable liability, with a reason.
11ObjectionThe objection is recorded (even if late — lateness is measured, not used to refuse), its admissibility decided, any deposit assessed, committee opinions recorded, and the objection officer decides: allowed, partly allowed or rejected.
12AppealA rejected objection can go to an external forum. Officers record the hearing and outcome — upheld, varied, set aside or remanded — and whether the outcome has been implemented.
13ReassessmentWhere a decision changes the figure, the case is reworked and goes through review and approval again. New information after a case has ended opens a linked successor case.
14ClosureThe case is closed with its final balance frozen and a retention period set; it may be placed under legal hold. Other endings are cancellation, write-off and time-bar.

3. Who uses it, and the checks between them

RoleWhat they do
SupervisorOpens and assigns cases, runs risk selection, records paper objections and appeals, records payments, reassesses, places legal holds, closes, cancels and writes off (cancelling and writing off always need a written reason).
AssessorPrepares the case: adjustments, information requests, calculation, submission for review.
SpecialistMay record adjustments on a case (for example transfer pricing).
ReviewerAccepts a prepared case or returns it for rework, with a reason.
Approver (levels 1–3)Approves or rejects cases in their amount band; rejection needs a reason.
Notice issuerIssues and serves notices for the office.
Objection officerRecords objections, rules on admissibility and decides them — the only role that may decide.
Committee memberRecords opinions on objections they are convened for.
Appeals officerRecords appeals, hearings, outcomes and their implementation.
Auditor (read-only)Reads the register, case histories, calculations and configuration; changes nothing.
AdministratorMaintains configuration: publishes rule sets and notice wording, deploys process definitions, manages the permission catalogue.
TaxpayerUses the portal to see their own served assessments, notices and account, and to file objections.
SystemCarries out automatic steps: evidence retrieval, approval routing, finalisation, notice generation, response windows, settlement and time-bar.

Checks between people (separation of duties)

  • The person who prepared a case may not review it; the preparer and the reviewer may not approve it. This is checked against who actually acted on the case, not against who currently holds it.
  • Routing to an approver and finalisation are automatic, so nobody can steer their own case to a friendlier approver or finalise an unapproved one.
  • Whoever writes a rule set cannot publish it; a second person must.
  • Only the objection officer decides an objection; a supervisor cannot.
  • A taxpayer only ever sees their own affairs. Which taxpayer a login acts for is read from recorded, dated authority on every request, never from anything the browser sends.
  • Every screen offers only the actions the person may take at that point, and the server checks every action again regardless.

5. Every possible case journey

These are the distinct ways a case can travel through the platform. Several can combine: for example, a case can be returned for rework twice, then approved, served, objected to and reassessed.

1. Straightforward assessment, paid. Opened, evidence retrieved, assigned, prepared, calculated, reviewed, approved, finalised, notice served; the taxpayer pays in full; the case is settled and closed.
Initiated → Data ready → Assigned → In preparation → Calculated → Under review → Reviewed → Pending approval → Approved → Finalised → Notice generated → Notice served → Response window open → Settled → Closed
2. No response from the taxpayer. As above, but the response window lapses with no objection; the case closes automatically.
… → Response window open → Closed
3. Information requested during preparation. The assessor asks the taxpayer for information; the case waits until the response is recorded or the due date passes, then preparation continues.
In preparation → Awaiting taxpayer → In preparation
4. Returned by the reviewer. The reviewer returns the case with a reason; the assessor reworks it and resubmits.
Under review → Returned for rework → In preparation → … → Under review
5. Rejected by the approver. The approver rejects with a reason; the case goes back to preparation and through review and approval again.
Pending approval → Rejected by approver → In preparation → …
6. Cancelled before work begins. A supervisor cancels a newly opened case, with a reason (for example, opened against the wrong period).
Initiated → Cancelled
7. Time-barred. The legal time limit for assessing passes while the case is still being opened or prepared. Automatic time-bar is off by default and never applies under legal hold.
Initiated or In preparation → Time-barred
8. Written off. After the notice, a supervisor writes off a liability that cannot be collected, with a reason.
Response window open → Written off
9. Objection allowed. The taxpayer objects; the objection officer allows it; the case is reassessed and goes through review and approval again before a new figure is served.
Response window open → Under objection → Objection allowed → Reassessment opened → In preparation → …
10. Objection partly allowed. As journey 9, for a partial change.
Under objection → Objection partly allowed → Reassessment opened → …
11. Objection rejected, no appeal. The objection is rejected and the case is closed.
Under objection → Objection rejected → Closed
12. Appeal: assessment upheld. The taxpayer appeals; the forum upholds the assessment; the taxpayer pays; the case settles and closes.
Objection rejected → Under appeal → Appeal upheld → Settled → Closed
13. Appeal: assessment varied. The forum changes the figure; the case is reassessed to match and reviewed and approved again.
Under appeal → Appeal varied → Reassessment opened → …
14. Appeal: remanded. The forum sends the case back for a fresh determination; it is reassessed.
Under appeal → Appeal remanded → Reassessment opened → …
15. Appeal: set aside. The forum sets the assessment aside; the case is closed.
Under appeal → Appeal set aside → Closed
16. New information after the case has ended. A closed, settled or written-off case is reassessed on new information by opening a successor case, linked to the original, which starts again at the beginning. Reassessing beyond the legal time limit needs a written reason and a named authoriser.
Closed / Settled / Written off ⇢ new case: Initiated → …
17. Evidence source unavailable. If a mandatory evidence source cannot answer, the case stays Initiated until it can, rather than being assessed on incomplete facts. It can still be cancelled or time-barred from there.
Initiated (waits) → Data ready
18. Legal hold. At any point a supervisor can place a case under legal hold, with a reason. The hold does not change the status; it blocks automatic time-bar and retention-driven disposal until lifted.

9. Roadmap: what is pending, in phases

The platform's plan describes phases 0 to 8, which have been built with the exceptions listed here. The phasing below is a proposal for sequencing the remaining work, grouped so that each phase delivers something usable. It is to be agreed with leadership and the first client; order and scope may change with their priorities.

Phase 9 — Production readiness and independent assurance

  • Tax subject-matter expert sign-off of every rule, rate and deadline, and of the golden calculation tests.
  • User acceptance testing with tax officers (the 12-scenario pack is prepared).
  • Independent penetration test; accessibility audit to WCAG 2.1 AA; performance testing of calculation, notice generation and concurrent users.
  • Deployment packaging (container images and orchestration), environment promotion, monitoring and metrics, operational runbooks; disaster recovery extended to document storage, identity and in-flight workflow timers.
  • End-to-end browser tests and the workflow engine's tests run automatically on every change.
  • Multi-factor sign-in switched on; captcha on public verification.

Phase 10 — Control completeness

  • Enforce the approver level required by each amount band, and the second approval at the highest level.
  • Write-off authority limits; assessor override of a calculated figure, with justification and approval.
  • A rule-set publisher may not work cases computed under that rule set.
  • Escalation timers for overdue review and approval; time-boxed delegation of authority.
  • Configurable "deemed allowed" objections and late-filing condonation; a disposal list for cases past retention.

Phase 11 — Connections to the authority's systems

  • Filed-return system, payment ledger and revenue accounting, taxpayer registry — each through its existing interface, so the tax logic does not change.
  • Digital signature and verification QR code on notices; SMS gateway.
  • Currency conversion where a jurisdiction needs it.

Phase 12 — Configuration self-service

  • Screens to author and version rule sets (rates, bands, thresholds, penalties, interest), deadlines, and reference lists and labels; user and role administration.
  • All eighteen assessment forms as configurable templates, including jurisdiction-specific objection and appeal forms.
  • Configuration promoted between environments as reviewed files, with two-person approval.

Phase 13 — Taxpayer experience and language

  • Portal: appeal filing, payment, replies to information requests.
  • Arabic labels and notice wording, with right-to-left PDF notices.
  • Engine coordination of the objection and appeal stages.

Phase 14 — Breadth

  • Further tax types — VAT, withholding tax, personal income tax, excise — and individual taxpayers.
  • Risk-model feedback (selection measured against assessment yield), officer productivity and further analytics.
  • Further jurisdictions, using the method in section 10.

How a new jurisdiction is added

A new country is added by adding configuration: no change to the platform's code is needed for anything the platform already knows how to do. The steps are:

  1. Currency and tax type — the currency and its decimal places; each tax type with its default currency.
  2. Working calendar — the weekend days and the public holidays.
  3. Reference lists — adjustment types and reasons, objection grounds, appeal forums, closure reasons and retention classes.
  4. Rule set — rates and bands, thresholds, loss rules, credit order, penalties, interest and rounding, dated from when they apply; published by a second person.
  5. Statutory deadlines — filing, payment, objection, appeal and information-response periods, each as "so many days or months from an event", on calendar or working days (and, if wanted, the limitation period).
  6. Deemed-service rules — when a notice counts as served for each delivery channel.
  7. Approval bands — the amount ranges and the approver level and number of approvals for each.
  8. Notice wording — each notice type in each language.
  9. Objection deposit — if the country requires one.
  10. Service levels and risk rules — internal targets, and weighted selection rules.
  11. Notifications and labels — messages and screen labels in each language.
  12. Forms — the tax return and, where needed, adjusted assessment forms, built in the form builder.
  13. Taxpayers — normally from the country's registry.
  14. Assurance — a set of golden calculation tests signed by a local tax expert, and a simulation of the rule set over sample cases before it is published.

The process definition is shared and takes its durations from the country's deadlines, so it does not change.

When code is needed. Only when a country needs something the platform has never done: a new kind of calculation rule, a new risk indicator, a new deadline trigger or calendar rule, a new delivery channel (for example SMS), non-Latin PDF notices, currency conversion, or — until those forms become configurable — country-specific objection and appeal forms. Each is added once and is then available to every jurisdiction.

11. Safeguards built into the platform

SafeguardWhat it meansWhy it matters
Exact moneyAll amounts use exact decimal arithmetic and statutory rounding rules; ordinary computer arithmetic on money is blocked automatically. The money component has 100% test coverage.A rounding error becomes a wrong liability on every case calculated after it.
Explainable calculationThe browser never calculates. Each result stores its full working, the rule-set version and a fingerprint of its inputs.Any figure can be reproduced and explained in an appeal or in court.
Golden tests42 complete cases pin inputs, results and working; a rule change must show an intended, reviewed difference.A silent change to anyone's liability is caught before release.
Permanent case historyEvery step writes one history entry in the same transaction; the database itself refuses edits or deletions of the history, even by an administrator of the database.The record of who did what cannot be rewritten.
Fail-safe accessAn action not in the permission catalogue is refused to everyone; an outage refuses rather than allows.A missed setting or an outage never opens access.
Separation of dutiesPreparer, reviewer and approver must be different people; figures are locked while under review and approval.Four eyes on every legal figure; the approver approves what the reviewer saw.
Configuration cannot run codeConfiguration chooses from a fixed list of behaviours; it never contains queries or formulas.Administrative settings cannot become a route for attack.
Frozen evidence and determinationsEvidence is fingerprinted and frozen at submission; a finalised figure can only change by reassessment with grounds.The facts and the figure a decision rested on stay provable.
Deadlines computed, never typedEvery statutory date is calculated from the recorded service date on the right calendar, with its derivation.A wrong statutory window is legal exposure.
Bounded time-barAutomatic time-bar is off by default and strictly limited when on; never under legal hold.A time-bar ends a collectable debt and cannot be undone.
Notice integrityEach notice has a fingerprint that officers and the public can verify.A taxpayer or court can check a notice is unaltered.
Taxpayer isolationA taxpayer's access is decided by recorded authority, never by anything sent from the browser.No taxpayer can see another's affairs.
Controlled exportsEvery export is recorded, scoped to the requester, rate-limited and protected against spreadsheet formula injection.The register cannot be copied out anonymously.

12. Architecture at a glance

  • One language across the stack (TypeScript) for the screens, the server and the form engine, so the same form rules run in the browser and on the server.
  • A modular monolith: one server application with strictly separated modules (platform, forms, workflow, tax assessment). Modules can be split into separate services later if load or team structure requires it.
  • PostgreSQL is the system of record; Redis holds the permission cache and queues; Keycloak provides identity; documents are held in S3-compatible storage.
  • Flowable coordinates work but decides nothing about tax: the server alone decides a case's status. If the engine is unavailable, cases are worked by hand and a reconciliation job reports any difference.
  • Designed to scale horizontally: the server and background worker can run as several copies behind a load balancer, with job locks so scheduled work runs once.

16. Glossary

TermMeaning
Assessment / caseOne assessment of one taxpayer, for one tax and one period.
Declared vs assessedWhat the taxpayer filed, and what the authority determines.
AdjustmentA change from the declared to the assessed figure, with its type, reason, amount and explanation.
Evidence snapshotThe source data as retrieved, fingerprinted and never altered.
Rule setA dated, versioned set of a country's tax rules used by the calculation.
Calculation traceThe stored, step-by-step working behind a figure.
FinalisationThe point at which an approved figure becomes the legal determination.
Notice / serviceThe legal document given to the taxpayer, and its delivery; statutory periods run from the service date.
Deemed serviceA rule that treats a notice as served a set time after sending, without proof of receipt.
ObjectionThe taxpayer's first challenge to an assessment, decided within the authority.
Admissibility / condonationWhether an objection will be heard; accepting one filed late.
Appeal / forumA challenge decided outside the authority; the tribunal, court or committee that hears it.
Upheld / varied / set aside / remandedThe forum keeps the assessment; changes it; cancels it; sends it back for a fresh decision.
ReassessmentA revised assessment — in place after a dispute decision, or as a linked successor case after closure.
Limitation / time-barThe legal time limit for assessing; a case that passes it is time-barred.
Settlement / write-offThe liability paid in full; an uncollectable liability abandoned with a reason.
Legal holdA block on automatic time-bar and disposal while a matter is pending.
RetentionHow long a closed case must be kept before it may be disposed of.
Separation of dutiesThe rule that preparer, reviewer and approver must be different people.
SLA vs statutory deadlineAn internal service target, and a date set by law; shown separately.
Golden caseA complete test case whose expected result and working are fixed, so any change is visible.
